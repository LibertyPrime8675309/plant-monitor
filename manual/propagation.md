# Propagation

Part of the Plant record manual. The README in the folder above has how to start, stop, update and back up the app, and the list of every manual page.

## Propagation

Tracks cuttings, layers, grafts, divisions, offsets, spores and seeds from the first cut to a new plant. The guide (see "Propagation and breeding guide") says how each way is done; this records what you actually tried. Open it from **Propagation** on the guide page and on every plant's page (**Family and propagation**), from the Today section when something needs a look, or by Search. It has no top-bar link of its own.

- **Start one.** You fill in:
  - the way: stem cutting, leaf cutting, root cutting, division, offset, pup or keiki, layering, air layer, graft or bud, bulb or tuber, spores, tissue culture or seed
  - the plant it came from (or none), how many, and the date
  - what it sits in, the setup, rooting hormone and where it is
  - how often to look. The usual is a week for anything in water or for seed, otherwise the way's own.

  From a plant's page the form opens with that plant filled in.
- **Record what happens.** Callus formed, Roots seen, New growth, Graft holding, Sprouted (how many), Lost (how many), Changed the water, Looked with no change, or a note. Each has a date, can be taken back, and starts the clock for the next look.
- **Treatment steps** (mostly for seeds). Soak, nick or sand the coat, warm and moist, cold and moist (stratification), needs light, dry rest. Each has days and a temperature range. The numbers offered are only starting points; every one is yours to change. A step starts when you press **Start** and Today says when it ends or has ended; finishing one offers the next.
- **Pot it up.** From **Ready to pot up** or at any time, **Pot it up** makes a plant with the next P-code. What describes the species (names, light, water, temperatures, feeding, size, safety) is copied from the parent plant; its spot, pot and photos are not. Its Notes say what it was made from. A cutting attempt finishes by itself when every piece is a plant or lost; a seed lot stays open until you finish it.
- **Family tree** (`#/propagating/family`). Each plant, what was made from it, and what those made, with generation numbers in words. Seed and plants from elsewhere are listed apart.
- **What your attempts show.** For each way and each plant: pieces tried, how many became plants, and the middle number of days to a first sign (roots, sprouts, a graft holding). Only finished attempts count.
- **Who can change it.** Editors; people who can only view see everything and no buttons.
- **Not yet:** finding attempts in Search by their name, Identify examples from seed photos, and counting days from a fridge or heat-mat sensor.
- **Since November 15, 2026:**
  - **Seed lots.** A lot of seed keeps when and where it was collected, and how many you **sowed** and how many **sprouted**. It also keeps a **float or cut test**: how many you tried and how many were sound. The ones that failed can come off the lot in the same step. Its page then says, for example, "21 sprouted (62% of the 34 sown)".
  - **Photos** on any attempt: the seed, the cut, the first roots, each with an optional line saying what it shows.
  - **Temperatures follow your Units setting.** A cold step of 34 to 41°F reads 1 to 5°C if you chose Celsius, and you type them in your unit.
  - **A plant grown from seed that came from elsewhere is generation 1.**
  - **Your phone gets one message at 8 in the morning** on a day when a treatment step ends or is next, or a cutting or seed lot is due a look. Turn "Propagation" off under Notifications if you do not want it.
  - A person who may change only some homes cannot change an attempt whose parent plant is at another home.
  - **What the Library knows about a seed.** When you start a seed lot, choose the plant it came from or type its species. The page then shows what the Library says about starting that species from seed, with a link to where it comes from. For example, a Shumard oak's acorns want 30 to 90 days of cold at 34 to 41°F, and must stay moist. A live oak's acorns need nothing, and should be sown at once. **Use these steps** fills in the treatment steps for you; change anything that does not suit your seed. A fact marked "Likely, not certain" says why. The first eleven are oaks (northern red, Shumard, Texas red, white, post, bur, live and escarpment live), Texas mountain laurel, Texas bluebonnet and butterfly milkweed. The same facts are on each species' page in the Library, under Propagation.
